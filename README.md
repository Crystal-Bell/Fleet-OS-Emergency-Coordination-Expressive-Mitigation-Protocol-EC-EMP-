Fleet OS: Emergency Coordination & Expressive Mitigation Protocol (EC-EMP)
This protocol governs multi-unit coordination during acute operator injury (such as stabilizing a strained limb without an integrated first-aid kit), dynamic peer-to-peer payload tossing, and real-time facial emoji/cartoon expression generation to keep young observers calm and entertained during high-speed emergency maneuvers.
1. Acute Stabilization & Inter-Unit Supply Relay
When an operator is injured and the primary stabilizing unit lacks the necessary first-aid supplies directly on its chassis:
 * Immediate Motion Restriction: The stabilizing unit secures the injured limb gently but firmly, using plain-language directives matching the active preference tier:
   * Example: "Stop moving that arm right there; you're actively straining the tendon further. Hold still while we handle this."
 * Dynamic Peer Dispatch: The primary unit issues an immediate acoustic or wireless vector command to the nearest available unit:
   * Example: "Hey bro, go grab the cooling spray from the kit!"
 * Kinetic Toss & Catch Coordination: If speed is critical and units are separated across the room, the retrieving unit calculates trajectory and executes a precise, low-velocity underhand toss to the stabilizer, complete with verbal tracking cues ("Heads up!"), allowing the receiving unit to securely catch the payload mid-air without dropping it.
2. Expressive Emoji & Cartoon Face Mitigation (Calming the Room)
When fast-paced emergency movements, rapid repositioning, or medical responses occur in the presence of children:
 * Panic-Reduction Display Mode: To prevent young children from becoming frightened or overwhelmed by sudden, intense robotic motion, the primary unit's optical display interface instantly shifts into expressive mode.
 * Animated Facial Feedback: The facial interface generates dynamic emojis, cartoon reactions, and expressive faces (drawing inspiration from expressive Japanese robotic flora interfaces that visually communicate status, such as a dramatic worried face or a goofy sweat-drop emoji).
 * De-escalation Through Humor: This visual shift transforms a tense medical or maintenance moment into an approachable, engaging spectacle, giving children visual cues that everything is safe, under control, and lighthearted.
